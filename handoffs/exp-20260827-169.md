## Proof And Tests

- Address/coverage proof: one formal `128x128x128` CTA stages `16,384 B` of A and `16,384 B` of B per K tile. Two formal CTAs therefore stage `32,768 B` of A plus `32,768 B` of B. A dual-M pair still needs `32,768 B` of A but only `16,384 B` of B, so B instruction bytes fall exactly `50%` and total A+B staged bytes fall exactly `25%`. Across case 2's 56 K tiles, baseline pair A/B bytes are `1,835,008/1,835,008`; dual-M pair A/B bytes are `1,835,008/917,504`. This passes the byte prerequisite, but it is only issued global-load volume: the current full-sort width-eight cluster already models eight A tiles plus one B tile as `8,257,536 B`, inside the 8 MiB L2, and prior profiler evidence measured about `86.65%` case-2 L2 hit, so physical HBM B traffic cannot be assumed to halve
- Accumulator/register proof: two `128x128` outputs contain `32,768` independent int32 partial sums. With 256 threads, every thread must retain at least `128` int32 accumulator lanes until the K reduction completes, versus exactly `64` for the formal single-M CTA. The current runtime-sorted clone already compiles to `20-byte stack / 254 MT / 40 ST / 32768 shared / 2 static warps`. The extra `64` unavoidable, long-lived accumulator lanes cannot preserve that register/occupancy shape. Storing one extra accumulator bank in LDS requires `128*128*4 = 65,536 B` before the existing 32 KiB A+B tile, and therefore exceeds even the lane's 48-KiB stop threshold. A global round trip of that bank across 55 K transitions would add at least `7,208,960 B` per CTA, far beyond the `917,504 B` saved B loads
- LDS proof: retaining only current A (`16 KiB`) and shared B (`16 KiB`) fits exactly `32 KiB`, but the second M must overwrite A after the first M consumes it. Keeping A0, A1 and B simultaneously is exactly `48 KiB` and triggers the stop condition. No 32-KiB arrangement can hold two A tiles plus B or hold the second accumulator bank
- Synchronization proof: in 32 KiB, each K tile requires `publish A0+B -> consume bank0 -> protect before A overwrite -> publish A1 -> consume bank1 -> protect before B overwrite`. That is four uniform barriers per pair per K tile, or `4*56 = 224`. Two formal CTAs execute `2*(2*56-1) = 222` barriers in aggregate, so fusion does not reduce synchronization and prevents the formal cross-K producer/consumer overlap. Removing either protection/publication boundary recreates the cross-wave overwrite race demonstrated by exp-167; adding inactive storage recreates exp-033's at-least-48-KiB path
- Grid/load-balance proof: the public skew distribution has exactly eight experts with 32 M tiles each, yielding exactly `8*16 = 128` full pairs and no singleton. A compact `8x32x16` logical grid therefore has `4096` active CTAs versus the formal `8192`, with every active CTA doing exactly twice the output/MMA work. Logical balance is exact, but half the CTA-level parallelism combines with the unavoidable larger live accumulator state; no grid remap repairs the per-thread lower bound. Completing M0 across all K before M1 avoids dual live accumulators but reloads every B tile and is precisely the historical adjacent-M serial-loop family, not the assigned mechanism
